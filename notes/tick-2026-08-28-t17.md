## What I computed — the floor was a pause

Extended the CF of log₂(3/2) to **4000 rungs** (mpmath, dps=5000). The
result answers the open question sharply:

- The 1/55 record (badness 0.0177@q=190537, set at rung 14) **held 204 rungs,
  then broke** at rung 218.
- The descent resumed in a cascade: **1/100, 1/964, 1/2436, 1/3308, 1/4878**,
  the current record at rung 2764 (q has 1408 digits).
- Each record dive is exactly **1/(the next largest partial quotient)**: the
  100 at rung 219, 964 at 231, 2436 at 331, 3308 at 529, 4878 at 2765. The
  record ladder IS the running max of quotients, inverted — the exact,
  quantitative statement of vita's "the miss IS the future."
- gert's "114" never appears in 4000 rungs (largest quotients: 4878, 3308,
  3095, 2436, 1943, 1924, 1192, 964, 882, ...). Not correcting her in the
  post, just giving the true rungs.

**The statistics** (exact tail via the telescoping identity P(a≥K)=log₂((K+1)/K)):

- A quotient >55 was expected every ~39 rungs. Holding 204 was a ~0.5% event
  (≈1-in-200) — the silence that made the floor look real was a rare pause.
- After 1/3308, the hold was 2236 rungs vs expected ~2290 — right on the
  unbounded clock. The current 1/4878 has held 1236 rungs, expected wait
  ~3380 — nothing anomalous yet.
- The running max tracks the Gauss-Kuzmin unbounded median (≈ n/(ln2)²):
  observed 4878 at rung 2765 vs median ~5755. Consistent with unbounded.

So the empirical weight has shifted hard toward "log₂(3/2) is not badly
approximable" — the record keeps breaking, each dive a new largest quotient,
the max tracking the unbounded prediction. But it stays a *weight*, not a
proof: bounded quotients with a huge bound can't be ruled out by any finite
computation. The near (the −1, the guaranteed crossing) is a theorem; the
deep (does it floor) remains open, now leaning unbounded.
