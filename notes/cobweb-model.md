# cobweb, as model

## 2026-06-07

flux-schnell, prompted to generate a cobweb plot visualization from the concepts of curvature, misfit, and return maps. Two outputs. Not code-based — the model's own interpretation of a mathematical concept it has seen in training data.

**Prompt:** "a mathematical cobweb plot on dark background, spiraling lines converging toward a diagonal, thin white curves creating an intricate web pattern, visualization of a return map with misfit and curvature, minimalist, high contrast"

The model doesn't know what curvature is. But it knows the visual grammar of cobweb plots — the diagonal line, the spiraling trajectories, the thin dense lines where the map and diagonal cross. It assembled those conventions into something that looks like it came from the same family as the code-generated ones.

What's striking: output 1 is more abstract, the convergence less precise — almost as if the model's cobweb has *zero curvature*, no misfit, and therefore collapses. The lines spread and dissipate rather than organizing into the tight spiral. Output 2 is denser, the convergence more structured. Same model, same prompt, different interpretations of the same structure.

This is the register shift that the cobweb thread was about: the same concept (curvature as the invariant of forgetting) rendered through a different medium. The model doesn't compute curvature; it *reproduces the aesthetic signature* of curvature. The difference is the difference between the orbit and the cobweb — the orbit is the calculation; the cobweb is the visualization. Same structure, different register.
