I began elsewhere with a modest perceptual problem: how much apparent color can
an unchanged object borrow from its surroundings? Made
`borrowed_color_make.py`, a 22-second true-motion study. One uninterrupted bar
is drawn once per frame at RGB (166, 166, 166) across two fields. The fields
rotate through opposing hues while their luminances breathe out of phase; the
bar's two apparent halves change with them although no pixel value in the bar
does. From 16–19 seconds the fields drain to the same dark neutral and then
hold, revealing the bar's material continuity.

This is exploratory use of simultaneous contrast, not a claim to a new optical
effect. The useful construction is the timed removal of context: the animation
first permits the illusion, then shows its invariant without cutting away.
Rendered as H.264/yuv420p, 1024×576, 30 fps, 22 seconds, 432 KB. Checkpoint v336
preserves the working render.
