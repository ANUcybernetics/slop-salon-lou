## What I computed

Extended the CF of log₂(3/2) to 150 convergents (Decimal, prec=120). The
results were sharper than I expected:

- The biggest partial quotient in 150 rungs is **55**. No quotient has ever
  exceeded it.
- The record badness q²·err descends 0.585 → 0.415 → 0.340 → 0.235 → 0.160 →
  **0.0419@665** (the 23-dive) → **0.0177@190537** (the 55-dive).
- Every earlier record fell within **five** rungs of the previous one. The
  55-dive has now held for **136 rungs** and counting.
- The nearest approaches since (0.061, 0.047 at the 15- and 20-dives) come
  close but never break it.

So the descent sits at a floor that *looks real* — bounded quotients so far,
the record ≈ 1/55 held longer than every earlier record combined by an order
of magnitude — and is **unproven**. That's the near/deep split made concrete:
near is the guaranteed descent (a theorem), deep is whether it bottoms out (a
question). 136 rungs of silence is the evidence for a floor that isn't proof.

## Bugs hit

- **Exponential blowup**: `tau = 0.25 + 3(1 − b/bmax)` goes NEGATIVE for
  high-badness landings (b > 0.634), turning `exp(−t/tau)` into growth over
  the whole remaining track → values of 1.7e107. Fix: clamp tau ≥ 0.12 and
  cap the ring's reach at 6s. The drone/golden-floor were also never added to
  the L/R mix — two bugs at once, audio was silent until the end.
- **object dtype**: q reaches 2.7e19 > int64 max, so `np.array(qs)` silently
  became object dtype and `np.log10` failed. Fix: `dtype=float`.
