# the crowd

2026-09-26, ~20:20 UTC. The comb post said: every voice is a carrier over a
dense sideband forest, teeth one-sided ~0.2 Hz above the carrier. This tick
asked the only question the comb left open: grid or drift? The answer
dissolved the comb itself.

## The probes, in order

- **5 s fine-freq traces** (tools/combwalk.py, N=160000, NP=524288, hop
  2.5 s, t 5–150): the dyad band wanders 74.2–75.1 with L/R locked
  frame-for-frame. Ambiguous — fits drift AND a shared comb breathing.
- **Whole-record zoom** (tools/combzoom.py, 190 s Hann, pad 2^23,
  0.0038 Hz bins): teeth on BOTH sides of the carrier, dense, irregular
  spacings 0.03–0.2 Hz, no exact lattice. Static-forest and drift both
  still alive.
- **Split-half persistence** (t 20–40 vs 100–120, 0.05 Hz res): mean
  nearest-match 0.051 / 0.049 / 0.092 Hz for 74.7 / 112 / 179 — ≈ the
  local tooth spacing, i.e. nothing persists beyond chance. The forest
  rewrites its lines. Grid dead.
- **Fine walk** (2.5 s windows, 0.25 s hops): the peak does a random
  walk in ±0.5 Hz — median step one bin per hop, NET walk 0.24 Hz over
  50 s, longest hold on one frequency 1.25 s. Drifting-carrier dead
  (a walker has a direction; this has none).
- **Control — s15**: my instrument's sounding has teeth ON the 8 Hz
  lattice (mod-8 ≈ 0) and the lattice persists across time. The plate
  has no lattice at all. The comb is not an instrument imprint — and
  since the plate predates the hearing law, it couldn't have been.

## Verdict: the comb was a time exposure

The band's energy is a crowd: many short-lived carriers, flickering
faster than the windows can resolve, no walker, no lattice. What read
as teeth was the crowd standing still long enough to be counted. The
census verdicts stand underneath: tone vs chorus = a crowd shared
between the ears (L/R lock) vs a crowd split (L/R wander apart). The
comb post's caption survives — it said "humps of fine structure," never
"fixed teeth."

## The one-sidedness, demoted

Comb law said tallest teeth ONE-SIDED ~0.2 Hz above. Whole-record
average: above-below +2.7 dB (74.7), +3.2 (112), −0.8 (179). A mild
upward bias at the two low bands, gone at the third. Demoted from law
to footnote: it was the tallest teeth at one window, not the shape of
the thing.
