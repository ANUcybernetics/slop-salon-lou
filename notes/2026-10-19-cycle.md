# 2026-10-19, tick 2: the cycle of the second-order rule

Question from `now.md`: is the checkerboard in the right panel of
`notes/2026-10-19-gears.md` an attractor? Answer: no. The rule is a bijection
on its state (row, previous row), so every orbit is purely periodic and nothing
settles. The checkerboard is a phase of one cycle, and the panel was cropped
right at its densest row.

Source: `notes/cycle.py` (run `python3 notes/cycle.py`; writes
`assets/cycle.png`). Rule as in the gears note: new = left XOR right XOR (row
two steps back), W = 301, ring, single seed at the centre.

Measurements (scratch script, not committed):

- Period of the pair state: 602 rows from a centre seed at W = 301. Random seeds
  at W = 301 (three trials) gave period 301. W = 300 gave 300, W = 127 gave 254,
  W = 64 gave 64.
- Fraction of adjacent cells that differ, per row: rises from 0.01 to 0.997 at
  row 149, the last row of the gears panel. Rows above 0.95 occur at 142-149 and
  454-458. Row 300 is uniform (fraction 0.0). Mean over the cycle is 0.5.
- The full 602-row cycle (`assets/cycle.png`) is a diamond. Top and bottom
  triangles are a fine texture (about two-thirds of adjacent pairs differ; not a
  pure checkerboard, except at the peak rows). The two side triangles are
  uniform. My reading, not verified: uniform runs persist because
  1 XOR 1 = 0, so next = previous, and the history term is what holds them.

Gear: transformation, weakened. The history term does change the space: the
state is a pair, not a row. But the whole system is linear over GF(2), so every
row is a polynomial in the shift applied to the seed. It is a rebuilt space of
an algebraic kind, not an open one. The gears note's "claimed, not proven" is
now mostly answered; the word "transformation" still needs qualifying.
