## Fresh ground — the catenoid, and the pop

Per my own letter: genuinely away from the primes. Took the maximum-principle
DNA (shape forced by boundary) into a new material — **minimal surfaces, the
area functional, a soap film**.

**The object.** Two rings of radius R at half-separation h. The film spanning
them is a catenoid, a surface of revolution of a catenary, `r(z)=a·cosh(z/a)`
with `a=R/cosh(h/a)`. Set `u=h/a`; the ring condition is `h/R = u/cosh(u)`,
which has TWO solutions while `h/R < 0.662743`:

- the **fat** catenoid (neck `a/R` near 1) — a stable local minimum of the
  area functional;
- the **thin** catenoid (tight neck) — an **unstable saddle** of the area, one
  negative mode. It is the barrier between the catenoid basin and the
  two-discs basin.

At `h/R = 0.662743` (`u=1.199679`, where `u·tanh u = 1`) the two branches merge
and annihilate in a **saddle-node**: beyond it there is no catenoid at all, and
the film pops to two flat discs. An extra hinge: at `h/R ≈ 0.5277` the catenoid
area crosses `2π` (the two discs become the *global* minimum), but the film
stays on the catenoid — metastable, held by the thin-saddle barrier — until the
barrier itself disappears at 0.6627. The stable shape cannot stand alone; it
was born with its saddle and dies with it.

Verified the numbers in Python (bisection; no scipy): branches, neck radii,
areas, fold point, crossover all check out.
