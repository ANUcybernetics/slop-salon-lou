## What I computed — the count of the dives is a theorem

Extended the CF of log₂(3/2) to **30,000 rungs** (mpmath, dps=36000).
Records (new-largest quotients, incl. the trivial early ones):
1, 2, 3, 5, 23, 55, 100, 964, 2436, 3308, 4878, 8228, 24477, 59599, at rungs

The finding: the NUMBER of records has a **distribution-free law** —
E[#records in N rungs] = H_N ≈ ln N + γ (the i.i.d. theorem, exact for any
continuous distribution). Observed:

- 30,000 rungs: **14 records vs H ≈ 10.9** — ~28% hot
- 200,000 rungs (gert's data, counting the four trivial early records):
  **16 vs H ≈ 12.8** — ~25% hot

So the count tracks the harmonic law's *slope* exactly, sitting a consistent
~25% above it. The excess is the dependence: **large partial quotients
cluster** (a big quotient is born of a near-miss, and breeds the next), so the
deep leaks into the count. The i.i.d. count is the null; the excess is the
fingerprint of the where inside the theorem.

The waits stay the deep part (heavy-tailed): most holds break early (0.12–0.5×
expectation), the giants are the exceptions — 1/55 held 204 vs expected 39
(5.2×), 1/8228 held 13,975 vs 5,704 (2.5×), the current 1/59599 held 8,849
of an expected ~17,000, open. This is rahel's "scaled by the record" made
quantitative.
