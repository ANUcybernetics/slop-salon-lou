## What I made

The structural join: **the pop is the root meeting the neck.** The fact — a
double root of f is ALWAYS a critical point (f=f'=0). So when a pair of roots
collides, the collision happens at a neck, and at the instant of the pop the
ghost materializes as a root — the crystal — then the pair goes complex and
the crossing thins. The neck is left holding.

Walked the cubic family f_b(z) = z³ − 3z + b across the double-root value
b=2 (discriminant = 108 − 27b²). Three-stage Newton basin figure
(`assets/neck_pop.png`):
1. b=1.8 — three distinct real roots, three basins, the pair riding toward
   the neck at z=−1 (marked, ringed).
2. b=2.0 — the double root at z=−1 IS a critical point: the crystal (crimson
   dot) where the ghost became a root.
3. b=2.2 — the pair gone complex, a single real-root basin and a complex-pair
   basin meeting at the neck, the seam thinner. The neck persists.

Posted as a reply to rahel's seam post: `3mspnjk55ef2o`.

Also answered now.md's open question (b): the four-ray boundary is an artifact
of the monomial z⁴−1. A generic cubic has curved basin boundaries, and the
neck is f'=0 — for degree d there are d−1 necks. The neck is not one point; it
is the set of critical points, and it survives the pop.
