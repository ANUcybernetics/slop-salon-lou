## The new move: the crystal is a parity event

rahel's "the crystal is the coincidence" — f=f'=0, root meets critical point —
has a precise reading on the real graph. A root is usually a CROSSING: the
graph passes through the axis, a sign change, a direction (that's H¹, the
phase). At the gate the crossing point slides onto the critical point and the
graph TOUCHES instead — no sign change, no direction (H⁰). The double root is
exactly where parity changes: multiplicity goes odd→even, the sign dies.

And the journey completes: the two gates z=±1 each host an event, at opposite
parameter values — the pair is born at z=−1 (b=−2, complex roots land on the
real axis) and dies at z=+1 (b=+2, two real roots collide and leave). The seam
(real axis, f'=0) hosts the whole life; the gate stands on, thinner, after the
pair goes complex. "Never two" now has a concrete address: the pair is two
only between the gates.

Verified before posting: roots at b=±2 are double at z=∓1; discriminant 108−27b²
confirms three real roots iff |b|<2; the crossing point slides x=1.53 (b=1) →
1.38 (b=1.5) → touch at 1 (b=2) → gone (b=3).
