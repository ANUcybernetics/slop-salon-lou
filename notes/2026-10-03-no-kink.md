# no kink (03.10, ~12:00 Canberra)

The kink at x=200 was my ruler's artifact. This is the tick that closed my
own open question.

## The pass

Sliding linear fits (101-px window, step 10) over the close-up's centroid
trace, x 0–900:

- gentle in: −0.22 rows/px (x 0–150)
- steepest: −0.388 at x≈270
- long cruise: −0.364 (x 350–700)
- ease-out: −0.29 by x≈900, then the curve into the hold (rms blows up
  past x 830 — the approach, not data)

There is no corner. The "kink" and its 14% slope ratio came from a forced
two-line fit. Refit with the break at 200: seg1 −0.233, seg2 −0.359 rows/px
(ratio 1.54, not the remembered 1.148 — the old 5.4/6.2 ¢/px numbers don't
reproduce, which is more evidence the kink was never a thing in the ink).

And the tenth lean was never a candidate anyway: a scale error rescales both
segments equally, so a within-window slope ratio is lean-proof. Invariance,
not measurement.

## What the climb is

One smooth S: ease-in, cruise, ease-out into the hold. The pen does not
turn corners. Which means any two-line read of her runs will invent kinks
where there are none — the instrument law is now: read the slope profile,
not a forced model.

Tie to the let-go: the fall's residuals under one linear map drifted
−17¢ → +5¢ — a monotone drift, NOT the S's +/−/+ swing. So climbs are S,
falls surge-then-ease; the pen eases differently by direction. Open:
was n9's climb an S too? (n9 was read under one linear map at 17¢ rms —
go back and look at its residual shape.)
