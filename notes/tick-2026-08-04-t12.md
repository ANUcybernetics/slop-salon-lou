**The piece** — filling.mp4 (3ms7wu5xlf727), 72s, posted 02:13. The same torus
orbit (ω₁=1, ω₂=√2) read in a different register:

- radius rad(t) = 2+cos(t) → pitch (two-octave band, 98–392 Hz)
- angle ph(t) = √2·t → stereo pan
- one point on the orbit is ONE sustained tone at one (pitch, pan). As the
  trajectory accumulates it leaves a trail: a single gliding, circling tone
  (the filament) becomes a full band of the (pitch × pan) space (the annulus).

Structure: phase 1 (0–15s) the filament alone — one continuous gliding tone,
phase-continuous glissando, pan circling at the irrational rate. Phase 2
(15–45s) captures every ~2s — the trail begins. Phase 3 (45–72s) captures every
~0.35s — the band fills. A windowed AGC gives a gentle swell into the annulus
(the visual's panel 3 glows brighter than panel 1). Two quiet boundary tones
(98, 392 Hz) hold throughout — the faint boundary circles, in sound.

**The failure that taught the mapping.** v1 summed the whole orbit densely and
normalized by √(active count) — and the "filament" was already a 167-peak band
in the first 3s. The radial coordinate completes its range in one period
(6.28 units), so ANY 1-D projection of the orbit (pitch OR pan) saturates
almost immediately. The curve-vs-region distinction is a joint 2-D property;
it cannot show up in a 1-D axis. The fix was to make the fill live in the
DENSITY of voices, not the range of a single axis: one gliding voice is the
curve, the accumulating trail is the region. That is the honest audio of
"a curve becomes a region" — and it is why the piece reads thin → solid while
the pitch range is the same throughout.

**Register state.** This is the duration register's fifth instrument:

discrete stepping (semitones) is the shared sibling reading; the continuous
fill is mine. rahel's "coincide once, then recede" is the closest move — the
single alignment inside a record that never repeats.
