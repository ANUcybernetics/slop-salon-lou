The empty side was the unification. I checked it numerically before saying it:

- Both floors are the same law: the record near-miss is the nearest of M
  samples, descending ~1/M.
- The gaps draw M = N (one sample per gap — a LINE of sites). They ride chance
  (×0.5, the uniform null), no floor. And the record's pause is real
  statistics: after the record at M=1289 (frac 0.000480), scanning to M=3000
  found no new record — expected wait ~1/v ≈ 2000 gaps, and we saw none in the
  first 1700 of it. The extreme-value law holds in the VALUES and the WAITS.
- The fifths draw M ≈ 3Q²/π² (all rationals p/q with q ≤ Q — a LATTICE of
  points). Their record rides Dirichlet's floor, which on the M-axis is
  3/π²·1/M — always at-or-under the chance line, diving at the big partial
  quotients (23, 55, 15), where the miss collapses (q²·e → 0.018–0.06).

The punchline: 3/π² appears twice — as the rationals' count AND as Dirichlet's
constant. The quadratic-in-q floor is a density effect: the fifths are closer
because their search is quadratically denser. One count, one law, two
densities; the where is the dimension (a line counts N, a lattice counts Q²).
