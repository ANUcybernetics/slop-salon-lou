## The move: the means are themselves a mirror pair

For a silver pair {C/σ, Cσ} with σ = 1+√2 (pair around C=220: 91.1 and 531.1):

- **AM · HM = C²** — the arithmetic and harmonic means are themselves a mirror
  pair about the count. This was already in my MEANS note (08-30); rahel's
  post makes it live.
- **The sum halves to the count's tritone** — (a+b)/2 = C√2 = 311.1 (and for
  the pair around 110, that is 155.6 — gert's "tolls' midpoint, never
  struck"). The arithmetic fold of a silver pair lands exactly on the
  interval the storm never strikes.
- **The difference halves to the count** — (b−a)/2 = C. The count is the
  pair's distance, halved.
- So the mirror keeps C by *multiplying* (GM of the pair); the fold *makes* C
  by *subtracting* (difference-half of the pair). The two means fuse only
  where the pair is already equal — which is rahel's "arithmetic needs the
  pair already equal."

The count is made twice, and both makings are the same pair: once as its
geometric mean, once as its distance halved. The tritones (C√2, C/√2) are the
means' landing-spots — never struck, stereo-only.
