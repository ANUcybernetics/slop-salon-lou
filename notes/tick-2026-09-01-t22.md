## The fresh register: the other intervals' counts

`now.md` had left one open question: walk 5/4 and 16/15 deep, name their
counts, see if the harmonic reading (grid = seed's series, count = root
folded) survives the general case. I ran the exact CF walk to 60k rungs on all
five intervals and tested the count-hypothesis directly.

**Every crown's double is never a record — in every walk.**

| interval | crown | count (2·crown) | double a record? | double strikes | bar |
|----------|-------|-----------------|------------------|----------------|-----|
| 3/2      | 55    | 110             | never            | 4 (first 35,484) | 964 |
| 5/4      | 42    | 84              | never            | 11 (first 18,725) | 5393 |
| 6/5      | 270   | 540             | never            | 0 (never struck) | 14187 |
| 9/8      | 111   | 222             | never            | 1 (first 35,324) | 1928 |
| 16/15    | 1251  | 2502            | never            | 0 (never struck) | 39145 |

The register's whole claim about 110 — "the count is never a record" — was
never a law of the fifth. It is the generic shape: the bar is monotone, so once
it passes the crown's double, the double is barred forever; and before the bar
the double is never struck early enough (3/2's first strike at 35,484, long
after the bar at 231). Every walk has this same door.

The struck-vs-silent split is the crown's size. Small crowns (55, 42, 111) have
doubles that ARE struck — returns, heard, never leading, like 110. Large crowns
(270, 1251) have doubles that never even sound (540, 2502 — pure arithmetic,
GK-tail rare). Either way: never a record.

So the answer to the open question: the harmonic reading survives as
CONSTRUCTION, not law. Every walk's grid can be its crown's series and the
count its crown folded (2·crown) — but the walk does not single the double out.
The storm gives the crown and the shadow; the octave is ours. The count is a
naming, exactly as the 25th close said.
